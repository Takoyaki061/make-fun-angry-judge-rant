# Candidates — Angry Judge: The Short Rant Contest

DeepSeek (100milionseek) was DOWN for the whole build: 5 calls, all HTTP 503
(3x angry-rant-1 prompt, 2x angry-rant-2 prompt; logged in ds-brutal.log).
All 6 candidates below are self-authored, then critique-picked in my own words
against the kit's judge criteria: (1) real funny rant about an everyday
annoyance, (2) actually funny, (3) specific/concrete, (4) sharp economical
wording, (5) freshness.

## C1 — Printer (REJECTED: freshness)
"The printer works perfectly — right up until the one document that actually matters. Then it blinks, eats the paper, and asks for cyan. CYAN. The page is black and white. It has held my report hostage for forty minutes and I am now negotiating with a machine that has the emotional range of a toaster."
Strong concrete detail ("asks for cyan"), but the printer is the single most
clichéd rant topic — the judge has heard it a hundred times. Fails criterion 5.

## C2 — Self-checkout (CHOSEN)
"Unexpected item in the bagging area. It's a bag of rice. I scanned it. You watched me scan it. Now a human has been summoned to stare at the screen, press one button, and walk away like a surgeon who just saved a life. I have been detained by a robot for buying rice."
Best on every criterion: a real rant, not an essay; the "surgeon who just saved
a life" line and the closer "detained by a robot for buying rice" are actual
laughs; hyper-specific (bag of rice, bagging area); every sentence earns its
place; self-checkout rage is fresher than printer/bus/Monday. 267 chars.

## C3 — "Circling back" (runner-up)
"Dave has circled back seventeen times. He has never once arrived. He circles back like a vulture with a calendar invite. If he circles back one more time I will print his email, fold it into a paper plane, and fly it directly into the sun."
Funny ("vulture with a calendar invite", paper plane into the sun) and specific,
but the middle sags slightly and the target (one named coworker) is narrower
than a shared everyday annoyance — less "anger feels shared" than C2.

## C4 — Bus (REJECTED: freshness + weaker punch)
"The bus timetable is a work of fiction. It says 8:04. It means: maybe 8:40, maybe never, maybe three at once like a clown car of broken promises. I have watched two empty buses drive past while mine is apparently still being assembled in a factory somewhere."
"Clown car of broken promises" is good, but the bus is another stock topic and
the closer is weaker than C2's.

## C5 — Monday alarm (REJECTED: freshness)
"My alarm went off at 6. I hit snooze. It went off at 6:09, and I swear it sounded smug. By 6:27 I was negotiating with a rectangle of plastic like it was holding my career hostage. Monday doesn't start at 9. It starts the moment you lose an argument with your own phone."
Relatable and the "sounded smug" beat is nice, but Monday morning is named in
the brief itself as an example — the most crowded topic in the field.

## C6 — Wi-Fi (REJECTED: weaker laugh)
"The Wi-Fi died mid-email. Not during a video, not during a game — mid-email, the one task a calculator from 1987 could do. The router is blinking red like it's proud of itself. I have restarted it four times. It has restarted my will to live zero times, because that's already gone."
Good specificity ("mid-email"), but the last line tries too hard and the laugh
is smaller than C2's closer.
